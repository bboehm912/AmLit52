The big question

What happens when a dream becomes too big to fit reality?

In Act II, work, family, memory, and ambition collide. Students will move from close observation to a defensible argument about the pressures shaping Willy and Biff.

Content note: Act II includes discussion of suicide and ends with Willy's death. Use school support protocols, allow students to step out or use an alternative passage, and avoid asking anyone to disclose personal experiences. Students do not need to perform or reenact the death.

Analyze

Character & conflict

Explain how an Act II interaction reveals a tension between a character's expectations and circumstances.

Interpret

Form & symbol

Analyze how a stage direction, memory, or object contributes to a defensible interpretation.

Argue

Claim & evidence

Develop a claim about aspiration using two specific Act II details and reasoning that connects them.

Preparation: Students read Act II in a school-provided or licensed edition before these periods. Provide a copy of the play, notebooks, and devices for the hub. Page numbers vary by edition; locate evidence by scene or act and add page numbers from the student's copy.

Teaching sequence

Two days of close reading.

Suggested pacing: two 50-minute periods after students have read Act II. Adjust discussion or written work for your class.

Day 1 · Reality meets expectation

0–5 min · Bellringer

When can a goal become a burden? Respond using a fictional or hypothetical example; sharing is optional.

5–15 min · Reconstruct

Individually complete the Act II Sequence Challenge in the Resource Hub, then compare choices with a partner and revisit the text.

15–30 min · Close reading

In pairs, examine Willy's meeting with Howard. Annotate one spoken detail and one stage or performance choice. What does each suggest about power or value?

30–43 min · Discuss & test

Play the Reality Check in the hub. Defend answers with a scene detail, then discuss how Biff's meeting with Oliver complicates his earlier expectations.

43–50 min · Exit ticket

Write one claim about work or status and support it with one specific Act II detail. Explain the connection in one sentence.

Day 2 · Memory, symbols, and argument

0–5 min · Revisit

Reread yesterday's claim. What might someone reasonably challenge or qualify?

5–17 min · Memory on stage

Locate the Boston memory and its relationship to the restaurant scene. Discuss how shifts in time and staging affect an audience's understanding of Biff and Willy.

17–30 min · Symbol studio

Use the Symbol Studio in the hub: choose an object, generate an interpretation, find a detail in the play, and discuss an alternative reading.

30–43 min · Build an argument

Draft a claim answering the big question. Use two Act II details, explain both, and acknowledge one reasonable counterargument. Optional extension: compare a class-selected Steinbeck passage.

43–50 min · Checkpoint

Complete and print the Student Work Checkpoint, or submit the same responses through the class's normal workflow.

Assessment & support

Make the thinking visible.

Success criteria

  • A debatable claim addresses aspiration, identity, or social pressure.
  • Two accurate Act II details are located by scene and, when possible, page.
  • Reasoning explains how each detail supports the claim.
  • A counterargument or alternative reading is considered.

Access & extension

  • Offer a scene list, paired reading, or a spoken rehearsal before writing.
  • Let students respond with a paragraph, conference, or teacher-approved organizer.
  • Extend by comparing a class-selected Steinbeck passage; distinguish similarities from important differences.

Game 01   /   pairs · 10 minutes

Act II Sequence Challenge

Choose the order of these events as the play presents them, earliest to latest. Then use your text to explain why the sequence matters. The Boston memory is revealed during the restaurant scene, though the remembered event happened earlier.

Game 02   /   solo or pairs · 10 minutes

Reality Check

Choose the best answer for each question. Check your score and use the feedback to return to the relevant scene.

1. What happens when Willy asks Howard for a local job?
2. What does Biff discover after waiting to see Oliver?
3. What dramatic technique brings the Boston incident into the restaurant sequence?

Activity 03   /   discussion · 13 minutes

Symbol Studio

Choose an Act II object to generate a discussion prompt. Interpretations are not automatically graded: support your reading with evidence and consider a different possibility.
